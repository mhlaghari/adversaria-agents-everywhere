How it works

Three steps. All of them local.

You start a recording, have your meeting, and stop. Everything from the audio to the finished notes happens inside your computer.

Record

No bot dials in

Adversaria captures your system audio and your microphone directly — the people on the call never see a recorder join. A live transcript scrolls as you talk.

Transcribe

On-device, speaker-labeled

Whisper runs on your Apple Silicon GPU and splits the conversation into you, them, and each remote speaker. Then the audio file is deleted.

Summarize

A local model writes it up

A local LLM turns the transcript into structured notes, decisions, and action items — picking the right template for the kind of meeting it was.

What you get

A full meeting workspace — with nothing phoning home.

Privacy is the floor, not the ceiling. Everything below is computed from your own data on your own machine.

Speaker-labeled transcripts

Me / Them, plus on-device diarization of the remote side into Speaker 1, 2, 3 — with [MM:SS] timestamps per turn.

Smart note templates

It detects a 1:1, a brainstorm, an interview, or a watched video and picks the matching template. Your manual choice always wins.

Meeting insights

Talk-time share, speaking pace, filler-word rate, interruptions, longest monologue — computed with zero AI calls.

To-dos & weekly recap

Action items from every meeting in one list — with due dates, assignees, a Today view, and a Mon–Sun digest.

Ask across meetings

Cross-meeting Q&A over your whole history, retrieved locally and answered by your local model — with the meetings it drew from.

Knowledge graph & export

An interactive map of your meetings, people, and topics. Export to slides, Markdown, or a portable bundle — on your terms.

Straight answers

The privacy questions, answered plainly.

Does anything I say get uploaded? +

No. Recording, transcription, and summarization all run on your own machine. Adversaria doesn't need network access to do its job, and it doesn't send your audio, transcripts, or notes anywhere.

Does a bot join my meeting? +

Never. Adversaria records the audio your computer already plays and the mic it already hears. No participant is added to the call, so there's nothing for others to see or consent-gate.

What happens to the recording? +

It's deleted as soon as it's transcribed. If a transcription ever fails, the audio is kept on-device and clearly marked so you can retry it — it's never silently uploaded to "fix" it.

What do I need to run it? +

A Mac with Apple Silicon (M-series) and a local language model for the summaries — the transcription engine is bundled. The private beta is macOS-first; a Windows build is next. Setup guidance ships with your invite.

Who makes it? +

Adversaria is built by Laghari Labs. It runs on open, on-device models — Whisper for speech and a local LLM for the writing — so the intelligence lives on your hardware, not behind someone's API.
